#0106 — adopting `swift format` as the Swift style gate vs. the recorded swiftlint budget

State before (unformatted, reverted after this measurement):
  scripts/lint-budget.sh -> 469 violations, 19 error-level  (budget 469 / 19, no new violations)

After `swift format --in-place --recursive --configuration .swift-format Sources/...`
(lineLength 120, indentation 4 spaces, respectsExistingLineBreaks):

  scripts/lint-budget.sh -> 327 violations, 21 error-level
  ::error::swiftlint regression against the recorded budget
    total: 469 -> 327
    errors: 19 -> 21
    closure_parameter_position: 0 -> 1
    function_body_length: 21 -> 25
    opening_brace: 0 -> 31
    trailing_comma: 0 -> 145

Per-rule after formatting (swiftlint --reporter json):
  trailing_comma 145, line_length 74, opening_brace 31, function_body_length 25,
  inclusive_language 22, file_length 11, ...

So the formatter fixes the rule it overlaps with (line_length 397 -> 74) but introduces 145
trailing_comma and 31 opening_brace violations and pushes four functions past
function_body_length, taking error-level violations from 19 to 21. `swift format lint` itself is
clean after the pass, i.e. the two tools simply disagree about punctuation and brace placement.

Resolution: the pass was reverted; `.swift-format`, `scripts/check-format.sh` and the CI step stay in
place, and the task is BLOCKED on an owner decision, because choosing between them changes the
project's declared style and its lint ratchet, which an audit must not decide unilaterally.
